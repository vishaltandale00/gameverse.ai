# ROM and ontology extraction handoff

Current direction, 2026-10-16: mine the whole GBA corpus for reusable mechanics, entities/pieces, strategies/skills, resource flows, assets/style, narrative and parameters, plus how the GBA implements them. Game composition comes later. The older root product handoff is not the current extraction task. The earlier detailed extraction handoff is retained at `local/HANDOFF_ONTOLOGY.md`.

Both supplied prior-work documents were read: `~/Downloads/The Composable Game.md` and `~/Downloads/GBA Game Space.md`. The latter is a text export of the viewer and omits its interactive data; use retained `local/embeddings/game_space.json` for actual results. Their research claims are background, not new execution instructions.

## Evidence and current results

- The starting corpus had 33 ROMs, 18 source checkouts mapping to 15 games, seven detailed game ontologies and no Ghidra function export. The seven ontologies were Claude source extractions; the 429 local-model profiles were title-only recall. These are different datasets.
- `tools/ontology/corpus_inventory.py` inventories all indexed ROMs, source revisions, capture coverage, ontology facets and missing stages. It does not infer completeness. Initial run: 5.4469 seconds, seven ontologies/413 components, 20 custom scripts missing from the recorded capture run.
- `tools/ontology/mine_corpus_evidence.py` deterministically mines observed hardware and source-file asset candidates without an LLM. Original captures produced 1,825 observations and 75,268 candidate file references in 6.6748 seconds. A second run over the fresh captures produced 1,829 observations in 3.9546 seconds. Both retain source/capture hashes. Candidate counts include untyped binary blobs and duplicates across source versions; they are not counts of unique reusable art assets.
- New GPT workers extracted eight additional source-backed and partial game records in parallel: Aria, Wario, Mario Kart, FFTA, Mario & Luigi, Ruby, FireRed and LeafGreen. A subsequent instruction-checked Crash numeric helper brought the corpus to 16 game files and 656 components; three later instruction-grounded records (Mega Man Zero, Metal Slug Advance and Prince of Persia) add 20 operators, for 19 game files and 676 components, including one added FFTA inventory rule. Crash deliberately has only one component; it is not a full game extraction. Sparse checkouts must not be presented as fully decompiled games. `local/re/corpus_inventory_current.json` includes the later experimental exports; the initial inventory is retained separately.
- Emerald's turn-order record now preserves integer modifier order, a Quick Claw exclusive threshold of 13,107, priority dominance and the correlation caused by a shared turn random value. Strategies inferred from these rules remain untested candidates.
- Direct source/ROM comparisons recovered another important boundary: FFTA inventory counts wrap to eight bits before the 99 cap. Source-valid argument values do not establish that players can reach an exploit. Crash's checked helper approaches two signed target values and integrates them into paired state, but its actor, units and gameplay role are unknown.

## Broader static discovery

Capture seeds alone miss most known functions. Against Emerald symbols, conservative push-prologue seeds took 13.3 seconds with 47.76% known-entry recall; adding bounded direct-call expansion took 14.7 seconds and reached 81.38% recall, with 99.57% all-ROM entry precision under the retained symbol heuristic. Exact function-body size agreement was only 21.91%: entry recovery is not trustworthy boundaries or semantics. Broad disassembler-valid pointer seeds increased false positives and were rejected as a default strategy.

Two bounded final-C pilots retained both outcomes: Crash generated 1,448 functions in 11.6 seconds, retaining all 751 capture-only baseline entries and adding 697; the earlier same-ROM/capture baseline took 7.2 seconds, with a different harness revision identified in the report. Warnings remain substantial. Driv3r discovered 3,236 candidate functions, then failed its hard 120-second limit during decompilation with no completed final export. No retry or larger discovery campaign ran. `local/re/experiments/static-discovery/report.json` retains hashes, configurations, warnings, calibration and stage nesting. Twenty focused offline Ghidra tests passed in 2.619 seconds.

## Loaded asset evidence

`tools/re/assets/` independently decodes bounded BIOS LZ77 uses captured by the instrumented emulator. The three-game pilot retained 378 uses across 213 ROM streams, with 371 first-use post-HLE memory snapshots matching independently decoded bytes and zero mismatches. Seven Crash uses target palette memory outside the snapshot reader's supported regions; those uses are not memory-verified. This is an observed-use channel, not a complete asset catalogue: raw loads, other compression formats, unreached content and semantic media classification remain unresolved.

Paired captures on identical scripts and ROMs took 17.83 seconds with tracing disabled and 18.49 seconds with tracing/snapshots enabled, a 0.66-second / 3.7% increase in one ordered pair. The 54 compared retained state/output files were byte-identical. Decoder time was 0.275 seconds; three decoder checks passed; incremental builds were 0.296 and 0.293 seconds. A later isolated Emerald capture verified 135 raw PC/LR/mode records and matching snapshots in 7.0 seconds. These raw addresses are not normalized call sites. Benchmark and later binary hashes are retained separately.

`local/re/experiments/20261016-loaded-assets/` contains the comparison, decoded manifest, raw-context verification and exact Emerald title-logo/tilemap source/ROM links. Destination and source addresses establish where bytes came from and were loaded; they do not by themselves establish reusable sprites, story content or gameplay roles.
