# CINDER RUN: readable combat prototype

CINDER RUN replaces Soul Loom’s inventory recipe with a short, single-camera combat encounter. Clear two guards and reach the visible exit using arrows, keyboard **X** to jump and **Z** to fire (gamepad A/B). Yellow charger windup shows its direction; the charger takes damage during blue recovery. The turret locks a visible aim line before firing. The central ledge and movement separate their threats. Three hearts, damage grace, clear failure text and X retry/replay make another attempt inexpensive.

The independent [quality review](review/QUALITY.md) gives **PASS as a bounded readable prototype**, not a claim that this is a polished full game, deeply strategic, fun for fresh players, or validated by human playtesting. The initial [diagnosis/design](DESIGN.md) explains why Soul Loom’s byte transitions and win flag were insufficient quality evidence. No browser gameplay was used.

Actual final native evidence, all on that exact ROM:

- Careless spawn-position fire loses three hearts and fails. X retry restores authored/public health, enemy energy, projectiles/timers and allows movement. Holding X through retry immediately starts a jump; reset position is transient rather than a forced stationary screen.
- The intentional route uses one ledge jump and ground pursuit during recovery. Both source energies change `4 → 2 → 0`, both source lethal poses become 98, and the exit unlocks. It completes at 218 game updates/537 emulator frames with three hearts, six shots, one shield block and four damaging hits. This known scripted route is about nine seconds of native frames; it is a short encounter, not a campaign.
- A ledge setup followed by exit-wall camping and sustained leftward fire loses three hearts and fails. The original recording already shows leftward bullets; an additionally authorized, unchanged-ROM replay directly observes facing and bullet position through a supplemental read-only profile. This refutes that tested camping strategy, not all possible safe strategies.
- Entry, windup, shield block, exposed hit, failure/retry and exit screenshots are readable at 240×160. The final lower jump keeps the player/aim line clear of the HUD. Scheduled windups are 30 updates for the charger and 25 locked-aim updates for the turret; actual native frame intervals are retained in action timelines, and update/frame rates vary with rendering.

Retry resets the authored/public slots. Opaque private MZM impact fields persist: private field4 and field3 are written by impact, while field6 remains zero in this invocation subset. Full source-private reset is not claimed; the relevant public energy/lethal/retry behavior is verified. Unknown private fields were never relabeled as semantic ports.
