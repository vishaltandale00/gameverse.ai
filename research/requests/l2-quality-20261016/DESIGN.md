# Quality diagnosis and proposed encounter

Soul Loom is a mechanics test with a win flag. Its native route repeatedly approaches three identical stationary targets and holds fire without taking damage (final energy 12), then executes a packed-inventory recipe. Player decisions do not change the combat strategy. One continuous floor makes jumping largely optional, enemies do not telegraph pressure, and there is no demonstrated failure/retry loop. The inventory objective is arbitrary and underexplained; its capacity demonstration can be skipped. The screen still labels itself ASTERION RELAY. Small crowded instructions mention an R control without distinguishing keyboard S. A byte transition and `won == 1` therefore establish compiler correctness, not enjoyable play. This diagnosis uses the actual plan, retained native facts/video checkpoints and rendered observations; no browser playtest was performed.

Proposed bounded successor: **CINDER RUN**, a single-camera 240×160 combat encounter. The goal is readable at entry: “CLEAR TWO GUARDS. REACH EXIT.” Natural controls are arrows, keyboard X jump/GBA A and keyboard Z fire/GBA B. There is no inventory recipe, shoulder-button mode or hidden exit condition.

The player faces two distinct threats in a compact arena: a shielded floor charger that signals its direction before crossing the floor and exposes itself during recovery, plus a slower turret with an obvious aim line before firing a jumpable projectile. One raised central ledge offers a movement choice and a way to separate their timing. The player chooses where to stand, when to jump, which target to attack and when to turn. Source MZM steering and Sonic gravity/integration determine movement. Source MZM impact determines actual enemy energy loss and lethal pose; the authored encounter retires a defeated enemy and unlocks the visible exit. Enemy scheduling, shields/contact tests, turret projectile, visuals, player hearts, checkpoint/retry and world geometry are explicitly authored L2. These are not claimed as original recovered Mario AI or automatic L2 recovery.

Feedback is part of the design: yellow windup plus direction line, red active threat, blue exposed recovery, immediate hit flash and visible target health. Player starts with three hearts and a readable grace interval after damage. Failure freezes the encounter and says “X RETRY”; X fully resets authored encounter state so repeated attempts do not require reloading the ROM. Success displays a clear result and X replay.

Available qualified inputs are `mzm.motion.accelerate`, `sonic.motion.gravity`, `sonic.motion.integrate` and `mzm.combat.impact`, with the existing source-backed ports/adapters. Unknown private lanes are not ports. No new backend operation is necessary for this authored design. Compiler owner is separately considering a connected Mario hit→reaction/retirement or projectile-lifecycle closure; it may replace a bounded part only after actual qualification, a stable contract and review. The encounter will not invent such a capability or wait indefinitely for it.

Quality gates before registration:

1. Every threat's windup is visibly shown before its first harmful action, with a measurable reaction window (initial design 20–30 game updates, configurable after actual native observation). Distinct charger and turret cycles occur in a retained native recording.
2. A careless stationary/ground-fire attempt takes damage or fails; holding fire from the spawn is not a universal winning strategy. A successful route must demonstrate at least one intentional dodge/reposition and actual recovered impact/lethal transitions.
3. Wrong-time shots visibly hit the charger shield without changing its energy; shots during blue recovery reduce source energy. Defeated threats stop dealing contact/projectile damage. Exit remains locked until both actual energy/retirement conditions are met.
4. Failure and X retry restore health, enemy energy, projectiles, timers and position. Retried play remains usable with arrows/X/Z alone. No emulator RAM writes or supplied checkpoint states.
5. Native screenshots at entry, windup, exposed/hit, failure/retry and exit show legible instructions, unclipped HUD, distinguishable silhouettes, terrain and no reused ASTERION label.
6. Reviewer assesses pacing, agency and feedback from native recording and these counterexamples, not just success flags. This is a short vertical slice, not a full-game quality claim or automatic design evaluation.

Expected validation cost: one fresh clean build (historical local compiler roughly 0.1–0.2 seconds; estimate only), up to three bounded native episodes: careless strategy, failure/retry, intentional successful route. Each retains the existing 4,200-frame/30-second native cap and build-stage 30-second cap; no broad matrix or new dependency/CI stage. Any iteration has a fresh attempt directory and retains failed receipts. Actual authoring/build/simulation/evaluation timings and unavailable queue/interactive duration will be recorded. No numeric latency improvement is claimed without comparable measurements. Do not replace the current Play slot until gameplay-quality review and registration ownership clear the successor.
